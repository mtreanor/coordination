## How agents are told what they get (the standing introduction)

Agents are told the incentive and the rules, never what to aim for: "What you get out of this is your own score, and the work's rules say what it is." (`OVERVIEW` in `src/coordination/run.py`, Nov 8, 2026). Any cooperation, retaliation or shunning is then the agent's own strategy, which is the minimal-interference principle.

- **Smith, V. L. (1976). Experimental economics: Induced value theory. *American Economic Review*, 66(2), Papers and Proceedings, 274–279.** The founding statement of experimental economics: the experimenter controls what subjects value by tying their reward to the outcome and stating the rules, so preferences are induced by the incentive, not by instructions about how to behave. The main citation for the wording. https://resolver.caltech.edu/CaltechAUTHORS:20171127-164410445
- **Hertwig, R., & Ortmann, A. (2001). Experimental practices in economics: A methodological challenge for psychologists? *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 24(3), 383–451. doi:10.1017/S0140525X01004149.** Economists' standard practice (scripted roles, repeated trials, performance-based pay, no deception) against psychologists'; argues the economists' standards reduce variability in findings. https://econweb.ucsd.edu/~jandreon/Econ264/papers/Hertwig%20Ortmann%20BBS%202001.pdf
- **Alekseev, A., Charness, G., & Gneezy, U. (2017). Experimental methods: When and why contextual instructions are important. *Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization*, 134, 48–59. doi:10.1016/j.jebo.2016.12.005.** Meaningful context (a software team, an investment club, rather than abstract labels) mostly aids understanding or makes no difference: support for keeping the works' framing while keeping the instructions free of norms. https://Rady.ucsd.edu/_files/faculty-research/uri-gneezy/context.pdf
- **Horton, J. J. (2023). Large language models as simulated economic agents: What can we learn from Homo silicus? NBER Working Paper 31122.** LLMs given endowments and scenarios, observed like experimental subjects. Not peer reviewed; the later arXiv version (2301.07543) adds Filippas and Manning as authors; the author discloses he advises Anthropic. https://www.nber.org/papers/w31122

## RUE's lineage, and why a symbolic social layer

RUE is the newest and most complete of a line of social physics engines: a symbolic social state with its full history, and behaviour chosen by summing weighted rules about that state into a utility for each action. Cite these four for the lineage, and the 2021 paper for the motivation. Each was checked against the paper itself (title page or publisher's record).

**Motivation.** If the goal is understanding (reading, interpreting and auditing why agents treat one another as they do), the social layer should be symbolically grounded and able to hold highly complex histories. Samuel, Treanor & McCoy (2021) make the case for symbolic over statistical AI: more agency, greater human interpretability, more controlled authorability. That is the reason this testbed keeps social life in RUE rather than in the language model.

- **Samuel, B., Treanor, M., & McCoy, J. (2021). Design considerations for creating AI-based gameplay. *Joint Proceedings of the AIIDE 2021 Workshops* (Experimental AI in Games), CEUR Workshop Proceedings vol. 3217.** The motivation: symbolic approaches "lend themselves to experiences with more agency, greater human interpretability, and more controlled authorability". Author order as on the paper's title page. https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3217/paper10.pdf

**The lineage.**

1. **Comme il Faut and Prom Week**, cited together:
   - **McCoy, J., Treanor, M., Samuel, B., Reed, A. A., Mateas, M., & Wardrip-Fruin, N. (2014). Social story worlds with Comme il Faut. *IEEE Transactions on Computational Intelligence and AI in Games*, 6(2), 97–112.** Comme il Faut (CiF): social exchanges chosen by weighted rules over a symbolic social state and its history, matching character behaviour to social context. https://cs.uky.edu/~sgware/reading/papers/mccoy2014cif.pdf
   - **McCoy, J., Treanor, M., Samuel, B., Reed, A. A., Mateas, M., & Wardrip-Fruin, N. (2013). Prom Week: Designing past the game/story dilemma. *Proceedings of the 8th International Conference on the Foundations of Digital Games (FDG 2013)*, 94–101.** The fleshed-out demonstration that a highly nuanced social world can be represented symbolically: Prom Week's social physics rests on "over 5,000 sociocultural considerations" (rules), over traits, relationships, a social fact database that is the characters' "collective social history", and a cultural knowledge base. The paper argues that representing interactions "that takes into account cultural context, personal history, and current relationships" would be impractical without such a system. In 5,425 play traces, stories had branched into 307 distinct paths by the third turn. (Pages checked against the FDG 2013 record and PDF: 94–101. The workshop draft's reference list gives 241–248, which is wrong.) http://www.fdg2013.org/program/papers/paper13_mccoy_etal.pdf
2. **Samuel, B., Reed, A. A., Maddaloni, P., Mateas, M., & Wardrip-Fruin, N. (2015). The Ensemble Engine: Next-generation social physics. *Proceedings of FDG 2015*.** CiF's successor, "the first publicly available social physics engine": a configurable schema in place of CiF's fixed categories, more flexible actions, more expressive rules, and queries over time windows of the social record. (DBLP lists it under an earlier title, "The Scored Rule Engine"; the paper's title page says "The Ensemble Engine".) http://www.fdg2015.org/papers/fdg2015_paper_07.pdf
3. **Treanor, M., McCoy, J., & Sullivan, A. (2016). A framework for playable social dialogue. *Proceedings of AIIDE 2016*, 12(1), 232–238.** Social practices, an extension of Ensemble: an interaction is a practice made of linked stages, each with actions, and participants take turns choosing an action at each stage. RUE's staged tick (work judgement → social actions → social judgement) comes from here. https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/12885
4. **Treanor, M., Samuel, B., & Nelson, M. J. (2025). Slice of Life: A social physics game with interactive conversations using symbolically grounded LLM-based generative dialogue. *Proceedings of FDG 2025*. doi:10.1145/3723498.3723806.** Ensemble with Social Practices (ESP) drives the game, and an LLM writes dialogue grounded in ESP's state, which is kept "deterministic and free from influence by the LLM". https://kmjn.org/publications/SliceOfLife_FDG25.pdf
5. **RUE**, the newest and most complete: full provenance on every fact change (backward-chaining justifications on demand), private stores per agent, staged action selection in tick plans, priming of ephemeral impulses, temporal windows, counts and aggregates, and reasons rendered for every option. Built by Treanor; pre-existing for the hackathon (https://github.com/mtreanor/rue).

## Authoring the social reality (limitations and future work)

- **DeKerlegand, D., Samuel, B., & Treanor, M. (2021). Pedagogical challenges in social physics authoring. In *Interactive Storytelling: ICIDS 2021*, Lecture Notes in Computer Science, 34–47. Springer. doi:10.1007/978-3-030-92300-6_4.** Why authoring social physics is hard to learn, drawn from workshops, collaborations and participant feedback on social physics projects, with a prototype tutorial game for Ensemble. Cited for why RUE's social reality is a first pass: covering all a social world needs is a long authoring job, and a skill in itself. Metadata checked on Crossref.

## The "voice in your ear": the social layer and an agent's self-image

The social layer doesn't decide anything: it is part of the prompt. The conjecture (Mike, Nov 8) is that a social world nuanced enough to tell a plausible story of the agent's inner life (how it feels about each colleague, what it holds against whom, what it was told) acts as a voice in its ear, and shapes how the agent sees itself, whether or not that story matches what is actually going on inside the model. The question for future work is what happens when that voice is part of training, not just the prompt.

- **Carauleanu, M., Vaiana, M., Rosenblatt, J., Berg, C., & de Lucena, D. S. (2024). Towards safe and honest AI agents with neural self-other overlap. arXiv:2412.16325; oral, Safe Generative AI Workshop, NeurIPS 2024.** AE Studio (a co-organiser of this hackathon). Fine-tuning that reduces the difference between a model's activations on self-referencing and other-referencing inputs (an empathy-inspired objective) cut deceptive responses sharply with little loss of capability, in LLMs and in RL agents. Relevant here as a training-time way to change how an agent represents itself relative to others, which is what the social layer does at prompt time. The headline numbers differ between the workshop abstract and the arXiv version; take them from the arXiv PDF before quoting any. https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16325
- **Chen, R., Arditi, A., Sleight, H., Evans, O., & Lindsey, J. (2025). Persona vectors: Monitoring and controlling character traits in language models. arXiv:2507.21509.** Traits (evil, sycophancy, hallucination) correspond to directions in activation space; personality shifts after fine-tuning, intended or not, track movement along them, and training data likely to cause a shift can be flagged in advance. A way to measure whether a social layer, in the prompt or in training, moves an agent's persona. https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21509
- **Betley, J., Tan, D., Warncke, N., Sztyber-Betley, A., Bao, X., Soto, M., Labenz, N., & Evans, O. (2025). Emergent misalignment: Narrow finetuning can produce broadly misaligned LLMs. *ICML 2025*; arXiv:2502.17424.** Fine-tuning on a narrow behaviour (insecure code written without telling the user) made models broadly misaligned, unless the framing was benign (a security class): what a model is trained to do shapes what it takes itself to be. Why training on a social layer's story of the agent is worth studying, and worth doing carefully. https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17424
